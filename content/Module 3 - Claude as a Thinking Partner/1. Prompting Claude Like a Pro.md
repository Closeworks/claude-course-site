The quality of Claude's output is directly tied to the quality of your input.

Most people give Claude vague instructions and wonder why the output is generic.

This is like hiring a world-class consultant and saying *"help me with business stuff."*

Here's the framework that fixes this.

### The C.R.A.F.T. Framework

**C, Context.** Give Claude the background.
```
Bad:  "Write me an email."
Good: "I run an AI automation agency targeting real estate companies.
       I need a cold outreach email to a brokerage owner."
```

**R, Role.** Tell Claude who to be.
```
Bad:  "Help me with marketing."
Good: "Act as a senior growth marketer who specializes in B2B SaaS."
```

**A, Action.** Be specific about the output.
```
Bad:  "Make this better."
Good: "Rewrite this headline to be more benefit-driven.
       Give me 5 variations under 10 words each."
```

**F, Format.** Specify how you want it delivered.
```
Bad:  "Give me ideas."
Good: "Give me 10 ideas in a numbered list. Each with a one-line
       description and difficulty rating (easy/medium/hard)."
```

**T, Tone.** Set the voice.
```
Bad:  "Write a post."
Good: "Write an Instagram caption. Conversational, slightly edgy.
       No corporate speak. Sound like a real person."
```

### Advanced Moves

**Chain prompting.** Break complex tasks into steps:
*"First analyze my competitor's landing page. Then identify 3 weaknesses. Then rewrite my page to exploit those gaps."*

**Few-shot examples.** Show Claude what good looks like:
*"Here are 3 headlines I love: [examples]. Write 5 more in this exact style."*

**Constraints.** Set hard boundaries:
*"Under 100 words. No jargon. 8th grade reading level. Must include a CTA."*

These techniques compound. Use them together and Claude's output goes from "pretty good" to "did a human write this?"
